\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This is a single, self-contained reference for the theory and mathematics of a
closed-loop, actively-learning electrophysiology experiment on retinal ganglion
cells. A multi-electrode array records spikes while natural grayscale images are
displayed; a Gaussian-process (GP) model with an arc-cosine kernel over a
structured spatial prior learns each neuron's receptive field and tuning from
Poisson spike counts by sparse variational inference; and \emph{active learning}
chooses the next image to maximize expected information gain about that tuning.
The document develops, in one uniform notation: the generative model and the
arc-cosine kernel with its structured prior $\Cmat(\bwid,\smoo,\rfc)$
(Part~I); sparse variational inference, the eigenspace representation and the two
current implementations, and the EM-style training algorithm with its E-, F-, and
M-steps and analytical/VJP gradients (Part~II); the information utility for active
learning, together with the predictive-conditioning, subspace, divergence, and
numerical results beneath it (Part~III); and guided diffusion, which extends the
loop from \emph{selecting} stimuli out of a pool to \emph{synthesizing} them by
steering a denoising diffusion model with the GP utility gradient (Part~IV).
Throughout, the current implementation is the ground truth; reconciled
discrepancies and open caveats are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:discrepancies}.
The treatment is restricted to the spatial GP (one image per input) and to the
current implementations; a deprecated legacy engine is out of scope.
\end{abstract}
